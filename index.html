About & mechanics

What is a Diamond PenTile AMOLED Simulator?

An AMOLED simulator reconstructs, emitter by emitter, how an emissive OLED panel actually forms an image — instead of handing you a smoothed screenshot of one. On a real AMOLED display every subpixel is an independent organic light-emitting diode: red, green and blue emitters switch on and off individually, strict black matrix gaps sit between them, and fine detail is resolved by the panel's subpixel geometry rather than by three tidy rectangles per pixel.

px.ali.et is a browser-based, pure client-side AMOLED simulator. Drop in an image, GIF or video — or author your own scene in AMO Studio — and the renderer lights every emitter of a true Diamond PenTile matrix separately, with tunable pitch, optical spill and bloom, so you see your content the way a modern phone panel resolves it.

Inside the Diamond PenTile Subpixel Matrix

Diamond PenTile — the subpixel layout used by most modern phone AMOLED panels — arranges emitters on a staggered lattice: green subpixels as small circles in a dense checker, red and blue subpixels as diamonds interleaved at half that density. Because green carries most of the luminance, a PenTile panel has roughly twice as many green emitters as red or blue, and neighbouring pixels share them. It is a different contract from the RGB stripe pixel of an LCD or LED monitor, and the difference is directly visible when you run a screen matrix test at high magnification.

PenTile vs RGB stripe — the density math

An RGB stripe pixel is three equal rectangles: three subpixels, each channel sampled at full horizontal resolution. Diamond PenTile averages closer to two subpixels per pixel (2G + 1R + 1B across every two-pixel pair), with each chroma channel at half horizontal resolution. Render a sharp vertical edge or 10 px text through an un-compensated lattice and you get chromatic fringing — red and blue edges where the source image had none. Real panels hide this with subpixel-rendering algorithms in the driver IC; this simulator deliberately shows you the raw, physical lattice.

Simulate Emissive OLED Panel Physics & Display Artifacts

The engine treats each subpixel as a physical light source rather than a filtered rectangle: an emitter response curve L = Iγ (default γ = 1.8) applied in linear light; per-channel peak output (red 0.70, green 1.00, blue 0.55); a Gaussian optical spill halo bleeding into neighbours; and a brightness-dependent bloom over the black matrix. Raise spill and colors re-mix optically before your eye integrates them; drop it and the raw lattice shows its honest, uncompensated character.

  • Chromatic fringing on text and hard UI edges
  • Subpixel grain in smooth gradients and dark tones
  • Optical bloom around highlights, separate from micro-spill
  • Off-pixel glow and true-black matrix behaviour

How to Test Text & UI Legibility on AMOLED Displays

  1. Open the AMOLED simulator and drop in a screenshot or export of your UI at real device resolution.
  2. Set the subpixel pitch to a target panel density — auto density keeps the grid visible on any screen, manual pitch emulates a specific panel.
  3. Raise optical spill and bloom to watch highlights halo; drop the pitch to judge small-type legibility and fringing at true reading distance.
  4. Compare against the built-in test pattern for pure grid behaviour, then iterate on font weights and sizes until your type stays crisp on the lattice.

Building animated content instead? AMO Studio authors layered .amo scenes — shapes, gradients, math and media — rendered on the same Diamond PenTile grid. The long-form technical breakdown lives in the AMOLED subpixel guide.

AMOLED Simulator FAQ

What is a Diamond PenTile AMOLED simulator?

It reconstructs, emitter by emitter, how an emissive OLED panel forms an image: every red, green and blue subpixel of a true Diamond PenTile matrix is lit individually, complete with the panel's optical spill, bloom and black matrix.

How does Diamond PenTile differ from an RGB stripe LCD?

An RGB stripe pixel is three equal subpixels with each channel at full resolution. Diamond PenTile is a staggered lattice of green circles with red and blue diamonds at half density — roughly twice as many green emitters — so neighbouring pixels share subpixels and each chroma channel resolves at half horizontal resolution.

Why does text look fringed on AMOLED screens?

Because red and blue resolve at half horizontal resolution and the lattice has no perfectly aligned subpixel columns, un-compensated rendering produces chromatic fringing on sharp glyph edges. Heavier font weights and subpixel-aware rendering algorithms reduce it.

Can I test my app's UI for AMOLED legibility online?

Yes — open the simulator, drop in a screenshot of your UI, set the pitch to a target panel density and raise optical spill and bloom. Everything runs in your tab; no upload, no account, no backend.

Does px.ali.et upload my images or video anywhere?

No. The site is pure client-side with zero backend — media, GIFs and video are decoded and rendered inside your own browser tab and never leave the device.
